# Narrative and composition patterns

Use patterns to solve an adventure-design problem. Do not force a pattern to
determine Package classification, chapter count, or schema fields.

## Scale profiles

| Profile | Typical scope | Recommended review points |
|---|---|---|
| One-shot | One session and one decisive arc | Ledger, integrated source |
| Short | Several sessions and a bounded ending | Ledger, integrated source |
| Medium | Several chapters or locations | Ledger, midpoint integration, final source |
| Long | Multiple arcs and consequential endings | Ledger, each arc integration, final source |
| Sandbox | Regions, factions, clocks, open routes | World ledger, region batches, final integration |

Use these only for authoring cadence. Classify bounded works as `adventure` and
continuing works with explicit endings as `campaign`.

## Space-driven patterns

- **Five-Room Dungeon**: guardian, interaction, setback, climax, revelation.
- **Node-Based**: connect locations, people, and events through multiple clues.
- **Hexcrawl**: prepare regions, travel procedures, locations, and encounters.
- **Megadungeon**: build levels, loops, access changes, factions, and rest pressure.
- **Island Design**: prepare independent situations that can run in varied order.

## Story-driven patterns

- **Three-Act**: establish, confront, resolve.
- **Hero's Journey**: departure, trials, crisis, transformation, return.
- **Double Triangle**: rise, apparent victory, fall, renewal, final resolution.
- **Kishōtenketsu**: introduce, develop, transform, reconcile without requiring an
  antagonist.
- **Beat Chart**: alternate developments and cliffhangers around a strong hook.
- **Hamlet's Hit Points**: alternate hope and fear beats.
- **Seven-Point Story**: hook, turns, pressures, midpoint, final turn, resolution.

## Play-driven patterns

- **Heist**: information, planning, execution, complication, escape.
- **Mystery**: revelations supported by redundant clues; never gate the only path
  behind one check.
- **Conspyramid**: layered conspiracy paired with an escalating response.
- **Faction Turn**: advance faction intentions independently of player action.
- **Race Against Time**: use a visible or hidden clock with explicit advancement.
- **Survival**: make resource pressure and recovery choices consequential.

## Character-driven patterns

- **Plot Point**: combine a main episode spine with optional personal situations.
- **Fish Tank**: prepare factions and unstable relationships; let players disturb
  them.
- **Technoir Transmission**: generate connected people, places, objects, factions,
  events, and threats, then follow the emerging triangle.
- **Decision-Based**: present legible choices and prepare consequences rather than
  one correct route.
- **Blorb**: prepare entities and procedures; adjudicate honestly from current
  state.

## Hybrid patterns

- **Iceberg**: expose a playable surface while preparing deeper causal layers.
- **Reverse Dungeon**: prepare defended assets, attackers, escalation, and loss
  conditions.

## Selection guidance

- Use one primary pattern and at most two supporting patterns unless the user
  asks for an experimental structure.
- Choose from the desired play experience, not the requested page count.
- Convert pattern beats into scenes, choices, clocks, clues, and endings in the
  authoring ledger.
- Preserve alternate routes. Never turn a narrative pattern into a mandatory
  sequence that overrides player intent.
